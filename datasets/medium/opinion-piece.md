# The congestion charge is the wrong tool, and I want to explain why

Let me be upfront: this column argues against the charge. I think the council
got this one wrong, and I want to make the case rather than pretend to survey it.

The strongest argument for the charge is real, and it deserves stating properly.
London and Stockholm both saw traffic fall by around 15% after introducing
similar schemes, and both directed the revenue into transit. Supporters are not
inventing that record. If you believe the same mechanism transfers here, voting
yes is a reasonable position held by reasonable people.

My disagreement is about transferability, not about the evidence. London had a
dense, frequent transit network before the charge went in. A driver priced off
the road had somewhere to go. Our network does not yet have that coverage in the
outer districts, which is precisely where the drivers who cannot absorb a flat
daily fee actually live. Charge first and build later, and you have imposed a
cost on the people with the fewest alternatives.

The revenue-recycling answer is the right answer in principle. But the council
has not committed to an allocation, and "we will spend it well" is a promise,
not a plan. I would find the charge persuasive the day it arrives attached to a
funded transit expansion with a timetable.

Until then, this is a toll that lands hardest on the people least able to route
around it. That is a bad trade, and I think the council should revisit it.
