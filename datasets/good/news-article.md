# Council approves congestion charge, 7–4

The city council voted 7–4 on Tuesday to introduce a congestion charge for
vehicles entering the central district on weekdays between 7am and 7pm. The
charge takes effect in January.

Supporters pointed to London and Stockholm, where similar schemes reduced
traffic volumes by roughly 15% and where revenue was directed into public
transit. "The money goes back into buses," said councillor Maria Alvarez, who
sponsored the measure. "That is the part people miss."

Opponents raised a distributional objection. Councillor Tom Reilly, who voted
against, argued the flat charge falls hardest on low-income drivers who cannot
easily shift to transit. "A flat fee is not a fair fee," he said. "Someone
earning thirty thousand pays the same as someone earning three hundred."

Alvarez responded that revenue recycling addresses the concern, though she
acknowledged the effect depends on how the council allocates the funds — a
decision not yet made.

Retailers inside the zone have also opposed the scheme, citing footfall. The
evidence on that point is mixed: London's retail data showed no sustained
decline, but smaller zones have less comparable data to draw on.

The charge will be reviewed after twelve months.
